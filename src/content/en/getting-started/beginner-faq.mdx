Short answers to the questions the game itself never explains, gathered from early tutorial coverage.

## Do I need to play the single-player story first?

Strongly recommended. It's under two hours, it introduces Michael's powers and controls one at a time, and it doubles as a tutorial for what survivors are up against. You can replay it on higher difficulties.

## Can I queue into a 1v1 or 1v2 lobby?

You can, but you shouldn't. The game is balanced around a **full 1v4** — wait for the lobby to fill. If the lobby chat turns hostile, mute players from the scoreboard.

## How does bloodthirst work, and why did I lose it?

Stalking, killing, executing and admiring bodies fill the three-tier bloodthirst meter. Higher tiers mean more damage dealt, less taken, and unlock weapon executions. You **lose** progress by standing in light, taking damage, or getting knocked down — so keep to the dark.

## Why can't I damage Michael while he's executing someone?

Michael is immune to damage during his execution animation. The correct play is to **shove** him to interrupt and rescue the victim, and save your ammo — shooting him mid-animation only wastes it.

## How do escapes work?

Five escape types exist: ground hatches, the car (repair kit + gas can + sedan key, then drive out of town), the police wagon (summoned by phone calls), the edge-of-map exit gate (bolt cutters or padlock key), and storm cellars with varying item requirements. Each exit only lets a limited number of people through before it shuts off.

## Can I come back after dying or escaping?

Yes. Returning as a **police officer** is RNG-based — it becomes possible once enough players have used phones ("police respond" appears). Returning as **Dr. Loomis** is guaranteed: activate the radio station once someone is dead. Loomis carries ammo and a flashlight and can help detain Michael.

## How do we detain Michael?

When Michael is knocked down, cops and Loomis can detain him — but it requires multiple authority figures around him (flashlights fill the detainment meter faster) plus skill checks. Even experienced players call it a once-in-a-hundred-matches event. Three stars is the maximum.

## What perks should I pick?

Spend perk points in the loadout screen, roll perks, and **scrap the junk**. Stamina reducers (like Unwound, −15% stamina use) are excellent; situational ones (like damage while drunk) are skippable. Equipped perks are the ones most likely to appear during your matches.

## Does stalking decay?

Yes — victim stock decays over time, but topping it back up is still worth it: level 3 stock victims are weakened, marked, and much easier to chase and kill.

## What does the golden noise ping mean?

Golden noise comes from a **main target** — one of the high-value special civilians. Blue/teal pings are ordinary noises. If you're Michael, chase gold first; if you're a civilian near a golden ping, you're standing next to a special target.

## Why did Michael suddenly vanish?

That's **shape jump**: in darkness and out of line of sight, Michael becomes invisible and moves fast. If street lights flicker, he's passing through them invisible. A brass lantern reveals him.
