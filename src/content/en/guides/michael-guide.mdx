Playing Michael Myers is a power fantasy with a cooldown bar. You get one starting weapon, six abilities — three unlocked from the start, three more as your account level climbs — and only **three ability slots** to fill. Choosing well is most of the battle.

## Loadout Basics

Before a match, open your loadout and pick:

- **A starting weapon** — pure preference. You can drop or throw your weapon mid-match and always fall back on the knife; whatever you choose, **the knife never leaves you**.
- **Three abilities** — from a pool of six. Three are available immediately, three unlock as your total player level rises.

New players need not overthink the early picks: the pool is small at first, and one ability below is never up for debate.

## The Six Abilities, Ranked by Use

### Reality Tear — Never Unequip It

The single most important ability in the kit. **Reality Tear lets you enter or exit Shape Jump from any state**:

- Standing in light, or someone is watching you? **Reality Tear.**
- Downed by police gunfire? **Reality Tear.**
- Someone is kiting you around a shed and you cannot close the gap? **Reality Tear out of Shape Jump** and grab them.

The cooldown is long. It earns it. If you change nothing else about your build, keep this equipped forever.

### Detection Pulse — for Hunters Who Struggle to Find People

Activates a large-radius scan that **marks players and NPCs** around you:

- Targets inside buildings show as a **red stain on the structure**.
- Targets in the open show a **red outline**.

The catch: marked players know they have been marked. Use it when the lobby has gone quiet and the [escapes](/guides/survivor-beginner-guide) are halfway built without you.

### Power Cut — the Breach Tool

A close-range ability that **kills nearby lights and cars**, including the transport van. It will not stop residents from entering vehicles, so it is situational — but it excels at two things:

- **Breach work:** plunging a house into darkness before you walk in.
- **Anti-flashlight:** shutting down the blinds survivors love to use mid-chase.

It also **forces doors closed**, which can cut off an escape route or seal a building.

### Shadow Strike — a Knife With a Timer

Throws a dark blade that **blinds and slows the target for a few seconds**. Honest verdict: it does not change fights, and it does nothing about someone kiting you around obstacles. A very short cooldown keeps it usable, and it has some value at close range — but it is the definition of a filler pick.

### Evil Presence — the Teamfight Answer

An area ability that **staggers everyone around you and reinforces your own staying power**. When multiple survivors swarm you at once — or you need one specific kill to land — this stops the whole group dead. Think of it as a slower, harder-hitting cousin of Shadow Strike, and a strong answer to groups swinging at you over a downed body.

### Shadow Mines — Skippable

After a briefly comic setup animation, you plant a mine. When triggered it **startles the victim and pings you a notification**. You can hold up to **three** and the cooldown is short — and it is still not worth a slot over anything above.

## A Working Default Build

**Reality Tear + Detection Pulse + Evil Presence** covers the three real problems of a match: finding people, surviving light and gunfire, and breaking up swarms. Swap Detection Pulse for Power Cut on lobbies that hide indoors and group up.

Two systems power all of it: **Bloodthirst**, your in-match momentum meter — reaching higher levels restores tools like Reality Tear mid-match — and **Stalk**, the mark-and-shadow game that rewards hunting from darkness. Feed both, and Haddonfield stays yours.
